01 · What we found

What we saw when we looked at Blinkist on social.

Before writing anything, we looked at how Blinkist presents itself today and where book content already has an audience. Four things stood out.

A big library

9,000+ titles in 27 categories.

Blinkist turns non-fiction books into 15-minute summaries, as text and audio. Money, productivity and personal growth are among its categories, and it runs its own German and Spanish sites. The summaries are written by people, not by AI.

The gap

The habit has to come from somewhere else.

Blinkist says openly that it is not a gamified habit app: there are no streaks or daily challenges. A creator can supply the routine, with a 7-day challenge or one book a day, and show it on camera.

One doubt

The trial lasts 7 days and asks for a card upfront.

Cancelling before it ends means no charge, but that is the first thing people want to check. A creator who walks through the real steps answers it before anyone asks, and turns the doubt into a trial.

Books already travel

Book creators have real audiences in both markets.

German BookTok has creators with more than 200K followers, and one was nominated at the 2025 TikTok Book Awards. In Spanish, TikTok reported 5 billion BookTok views. That audience is already used to being told what to read next.

04 · A wildcard worth testing

Four creator types nobody expects from a book app.

Next to the shortlist, we'd keep a small part of the first wave for four creator types outside the usual book crowd. A few creators each, measured like the rest. If one works, it becomes a channel of its own.

Wildcard 1

Animated book stories

Illustrators who tell the idea of one book per episode with a recurring character. It suits Blinkist because every Blink is already one idea in 15 minutes, and a drawn story is brand-safe in any market. It's a format that worked very well for us with Flo.

"One book, one drawing: what Atomic Habits did to my Monday."
#animation · #bookstories · #animación
Wildcard 2

Hands busy, ears free

Truck drivers, delivery riders and tradespeople who listen to a Blink on the job. Blinkist already pitches audio for driving, chores and commuting, and these creators live that moment every day.

"Eight hours on the road, four books in my ears."
#trucklife · #repartidor · #handwerker
Wildcard 3

Night-feed parents

New parents who use the quiet 15 minutes of a night feed to learn something. It speaks to a very specific moment, and Blinkist has parenting and mindset titles ready for it.

"3 a.m., bottle in one hand, a book in my ears."
#nightfeed · #newparents · #mamaprimeriza
Wildcard 4

Books everyone pretends to have read

Comedy creators with sketches about the books we all say we have read. Each one ends with a 15-minute way to actually hold the conversation. Humour breaks the self-improvement tone and travels well in both languages.

"How to survive a dinner with people who read Sapiens."
#booktok · #humor · #comedia
How we'd test it: two or three creators per wildcard inside the first wave, each with their own link, so you can compare cost per trial against the main shortlist. We'd scout these creators after our call.
05 · A first wave

How a first wave would work.

Start small, measure trials per creator and keep only what works. We'd agree budget and timing together after a call, once we know what matters most to you.

1

Brief 6–10 creators

Picked from the list above, across sizes and split between both markets. Each one gets a brief, a unique link and the hooks to test.

2

Read trials per creator

After two to three weeks: trials, cost per trial and the winning hook in each market. Tracked with a unique link per creator, UTMs and your attribution setup.

3

Scale what works

Boost the best videos as Spark Ads and partnership ads, re-brief the creators who convert and swap the rest. The next wave starts from proof.
